Source of truth for the pilot numbers: saturation_diag.txt (this dir), produced by
alpha_saturation_diag.py (this dir) on 9.5_tuning's own pilot rows / features.

Why not 9.5_tuning's recommended_alpha (3.948528, run3/outputs/9.5_tuning/alpha_calibration.txt):
  - recommend_alpha() requires sat_frac <= 0.25, where sat_frac counts ALL steered
    outputs with P<0.02 or P>0.98, including ones already saturated at baseline.
  - Measured SAE-recon baseline saturation on the pilot rows is 0.325 (> 0.25), so
    no alpha can pass; the function fell to its last-resort branch (max mean|dP|
    over all rows), which selects the largest, most saturated alpha (sat 0.565).
  - Extending the grid downward cannot help: sat_tot >= 0.330 at every alpha tested,
    down to 1/32 x median scale.
  - Contributing cause (not fixed): the layer-11 residual probe is fit on train and
    the pilot rows are train rows (clean-forward accuracy on them 0.945, sat 0.475).

Decision (approved by user: "relax the cap or expand the grid, your call"):
  - Relax the saturation criterion to NEWLY saturated (saturated after steering but
    not at baseline) <= 0.25; keep 9.5_tuning's original grid {0.5,1,2,4} x median
    scale and its mean|dP| >= 0.02 criterion.
  - Smallest eligible alpha: 0.493566 (x0.5): mean|dP|=0.0490, sat_new=0.028.
  - Not chosen: extended-grid 0.2468 (x0.25) also passes, but mean|dP|=0.0246 is
    marginal against 0.02 and the steer scripts use a different (logit-diff) readout.
  - Same CLAUDE.md caveat applies: alpha is a rough "measurable but unsaturated"
    magnitude calibrated on a layer-11 probe, not a tuned hyperparameter.
