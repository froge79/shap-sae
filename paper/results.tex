As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:mlp-ranking-agreement} and Table~\ref{tab:app-mlp-ranking}, with all three methods aggregated the same way (mean $|\cdot|$ over the 1000 evaluation rows, full-training baseline), IG, GA and SHAP agree almost perfectly on global importance (IG--SHAP $\rho=0.995$, GA--SHAP $0.984$, IG--GA $0.980$), and probe saliency correlates only weakly with all three ($0.31$--$0.34$). \subsection{Causal Faithfulness}
Table~\ref{tab:all-selection-faithfulness-last} reports global faithfulness across all 114 masked features under last-token steering, BH-corrected within arm. SHAP importance is mean $|\phi|$ against the full training set; SHAP direction is the signed mean $\Phi$. SHAP, IG, and GA are scored with the same aggregation (mean absolute attribution over the evaluation rows, full-training baseline); in the linear arm they are then identical by construction, and in the MLP arm they differ by at most $0.02$ (last-token). Importance faithfulness is statistically significant for all three in both arms but small-to-moderate in size ($\rho_{\text{imp}} = 0.42$--$0.46$, $q \le 10^{-5}$; $\rho^2\approx0.18$--$0.21$), so each method explains only a modest share of the variation in feature effects. The zero-cost baseline is below all three in the linear arm (probe weights, $\rho_{\text{imp}} = 0.270$) and far below them in the MLP arm (saliency, $\rho_{\text{imp}} = 0.032$). Directional faithfulness is weaker: SHAP, IG, and GA are near zero and nonsignificant in both arms, while probe weights alone reach $\rho_{\text{dir}} = 0.305$ in the linear arm ($0.399$ under all-token steering). The weights beat SHAP by $0.41$ (95\% CI $[0.09,0.72]$, resampling the 114 features; $0.50$ $[0.17,0.79]$ all-token), so a method built on the weights predicts the direction of intervention effects worse than the weights themselves. This is notable because attribution adds instance-specific information (the feature’s activation relative to the baseline) to the same weights, yet predicts the direction of intervention effects worse than the weights do. Steering adds a fixed shift, so the sign of its effect follows the sign of the weight, whereas attribution multiplies each weight by a deviation $(x_j - \text{baseline}_j)$ that can change sign. The probe-side controls below test this: removing the background restores the direction. Overall, 5/8 linear-arm and 3/7 MLP-arm tests survive FDR correction (the five linear survivors count SHAP, IG and GA separately; they have identical $p$-values by construction, so 3/4 distinct tests). The importance and directional conclusions do not depend on how the test family is defined (Appendix~\ref{app:bh}). Pooling every global test (both arms, both scopes, duplicates removed) into one family leaves the same 12 of 22 tests significant. Pooling all 30 inferential tests in the paper into one family gives 13 survivors, the 12 global tests and one local test (Section~\ref{subsection:local_faithfulness}). Under Benjamini--Yekutieli, which allows arbitrary dependence, the same 12 and 13 survive. No directional test of SHAP, IG, or GA has $q<0.25$ under any family.

All-token steering gives higher importance faithfulness than last-token steering for SHAP, IG and GA in the MLP arm ($0.476$--$0.500$ vs.\ $0.423$--$0.444$); in the linear arm the two scopes are close ($0.430$ vs.\ $0.455$). The same tests survive FDR correction in both scopes (Appendix~\ref{app:all-token-global-faithfulness}). Matching the intervention scope to the attributed token position therefore does not improve measured faithfulness.

\paragraph{Global score and background.} Scoring importance with the signed mean $\Phi$ instead of mean $|\phi|$ lowers importance faithfulness (linear $0.303$ vs.\ $0.455$ last-token, $0.377$ vs.\ $0.430$ all-token; MLP $0.233$ vs.\ $0.425$, $0.347$ vs.\ $0.476$). The same holds for IG and GA, whose signed means gave $0.303$/$0.377$ (linear) and $0.32$--$0.44$ (MLP): the earlier apparent advantage of SHAP over IG and GA came from this aggregation, not from the method. Signed-mean scoring also makes importance depend on the background draw (95\% range about $0.2$ wide over bootstrapped 100-row backgrounds, against at most $0.04$ for mean $|\phi|$; Appendix~\ref{app:global-baseline}). The background does not change the directional result: $\Phi$'s directional $\rho$ stays near zero under the full training set and under bootstrapped backgrounds.



\paragraph{Probe-side controls.} Because attributions explain the probe and interventions act on the language model, a low $\rho$ could reflect either. For the linear probe we can score attributions against the probe's own closed-form response to the intervention (Section~\ref{para:probe-controls}, Appendix~\ref{app:probe-controls}). Against the probe's steering effect, signed $\Phi$ is anti-correlated ($\rho_{\text{dir}}=-0.28$ with the 100-row background, $-0.37$ with the full training set), while the background-free product $w_j\bar{x}_j$ reaches $+0.95$. The directional failure is therefore attribution error relative to the probe, produced by centring on a background mean, and does not require any probe--LM mismatch. A class-matched background does not change it (sign agreement with the weight $43.0\%$ and $\rho_{\text{dir}}=-0.15$ under both the matched and the full-training background; stratified 100-row backgrounds give $-0.06$ $[-0.26,+0.15]$ against $-0.05$ $[-0.23,+0.17]$ unstratified), and the class-balance explanation is excluded. The probe--LM link is itself only moderate: the probe's steering effect predicts the measured logit-difference effect with $\rho=+0.31$ (95\% CI $[+0.10,+0.49]$, resampling features), so the language-model test has limited resolution. At that level the background-free product ($\rho_{\text{dir}}=+0.35$) and the raw weight ($+0.31$) do better than $\Phi$ ($-0.11$) in direction, and in importance the background-free product ($0.47$) is as high as mean $|\phi|$ ($0.46$), with $\Phi$ ($0.30$) and the weight ($0.27$) lower, so SHAP importance does not beat a baseline with no background. The same holds under all-token steering and under ablating all 114 features: the probe's own ablation effect $-\mathbb{E}_e[w_jx_{ej}]$ predicts the measured effect with $\rho=+0.34$ (95\% CI $[+0.12,+0.54]$, last-token; $+0.35$ all-token), and the raw weight ($+0.29$) does better than $\Phi$ ($-0.12$). We therefore read the global directional null as a property of $\Phi$ rather than evidence about the language model, and the importance results as showing that SHAP is no better than simple probe-based scores. The design cannot show that any probe-based score is unfaithful to the language model beyond the level set by the probe--LM link.

\subsection{Local Faithfulness}
Figure~\ref{fig:local-vs-global} outlines local vs. global SHAP causal faithfulness results, with global SHAP scored by mean $|\phi|$. Our headline comparison is Block B (active features only): ablating an inactive feature moves the logit by exactly zero while local $|\phi(x)|$ tracks activity by construction, so Block A is dominated by a tied block. With the control (Block B), the importance gap is negative in both arms: nominally significant in the linear arm ($-0.305$, $p=0.036$, but $q=0.14$) and not in the MLP arm ($-0.213$, $p=0.29$). No directional gap is significant in either block. Without the control (Block A), local SHAP ranks importance better than the global score in the MLP arm (gap $+0.389$, $p=0.002$, surviving FDR correction with $q=0.008$ within the local family) but not significantly in the linear arm ($+0.161$, $p=0.21$). This is the one local result that survives correction, and it is fragile: rescoring the original candidate sets (built before the switch to mean $|\phi|$) with a 100-row background gives $+0.222$ with $p=0.054$ (Appendix~\ref{app:global-baseline}). Local SHAP therefore ranks features by effect better than the global score only when inactive features are included, where it benefits from knowing which features are active; among active features we find no evidence that it is more faithful, and the point estimates favour the global score. Replacing the earlier signed-$\Phi$ baseline with mean $|\phi|$ roughly halves the linear-arm Block A gap ($+0.294\to+0.161$) and leaves the MLP-arm gap in place (Appendix~\ref{app:global-baseline}).

In Block B, restricting to active features reverses the importance gap in both arms ($-0.305$ and $-0.213$); only the linear arm's crossed-bootstrap CI excludes zero, and that gap does not survive FDR correction. In Block A, local attribution scored higher than global attribution on importance faithfulness (gap $+0.161$ linear, $p=0.21$; $+0.389$ MLP, $p=0.002$), with no directional difference. The active fraction of candidates explained a modest share of the between-example variation in the Block A importance gap ($R^2=0.105$ linear, $0.080$ MLP; $0.122$ and $0.189$ controlling for candidate-set size).

\paragraph{Why global directional attribution may fail.} For a linear probe, $\Phi_j = w_j(\bar{x}_j^{\text{eval}} - \mathbb{E}_{\text{bg}}[x_j])$, so global attribution mostly reflects how the evaluation rows differ from the background rather than feature importance. Its magnitude tracks the size of this mean shift more closely than the size of the weights ($\rho = 0.85$ vs.\ $0.42$ over the non-zero-weight features), and summed over features it totals only $+0.24$ logits. Only 40\% of the top-20 $|\Phi|$ features (among all non-zero-weight features) have $\Phi$ matching the sign of their weight, and 25\% within the 114-feature masked pool. The background's class balance (49\% positive versus 51.5\% in the evaluation rows) does not predict these sign mismatches (matching it exactly leaves them unchanged, above), and a larger background does not remove them: against all 6{,}696 training rows, $\Phi$ matches the weight's sign for 43\% of the 114 masked features (46\% with the 100-row background), and directional $\rho$ over 500 bootstrapped 100-row backgrounds is centred on zero ($-0.03$, 95\% range $[-0.21,+0.17]$; linear arm, all-token). Because the evaluation and background rows are drawn from the same distribution, the shift $\bar{x}_j^{\text{eval}}-\mathbb{E}_{\text{bg}}[x_j]$ has no reason to share the weight's sign, so the sign of $\Phi$ says little about the weight. It is not pure noise, though: against the full training set the shift is negatively related to a feature's training mean ($\rho=-0.21$) and to its activation scale ($\rho=-0.25$), and $57\%$ of the 114 features have a negative shift (held-out means sit slightly below training means, consistent with selection on the training rows). Because the steering size $\alpha_j$ scales with a feature's activation scale, this makes $\Phi$ anti-correlated with the probe's steering effect ($-0.37$, above). This follows from defining $\Phi$ as a signed mean, not from the size of the background. Dropping the background altogether ($w_j\bar{x}_j$) restores a positive directional $\rho$, which supports this as the explanation of the near-zero global directional faithfulness.

\subsection{Known Ground-Truth Validation and Lexical Simplicity Control}
Table~\ref{tab:minimal-pairs} reports 100 minimal pairs for each of four edits that cross type with position relative to the final token. The readout tracks the known direction in all four (sign accuracy 0.76--0.96), but it is weakest for removal and interior insertion, where effects sit close to a null-edit noise floor (Appendix~\ref{app:minimal-pairs}). Against random distractors, local SHAP is modestly above chance (AUROC 0.49--0.62) and global SHAP, which equals IG and GA once all are aggregated as mean $|\cdot|$ (linear arm), is near chance (0.42--0.51). The apparent insertion advantage for local SHAP is a position effect: appending the clause (0.622) beats placing it mid-sentence (0.492), while removal and interior insertion are similar (0.553 vs.\ 0.492 in this table, $+0.01\pm0.02$ over seeds 0--3). Same-seed differences over seeds 0--3 agree (insertion minus interior insertion $+0.12 \pm 0.03$, positive in 4/4; removal minus interior insertion $+0.01 \pm 0.02$). Feature recovery is also not shown to exceed what activity alone gives. The attribution-free baseline ``active at the edited last token'' reaches AUROC 0.79--0.85, above every method, and once distractors are activity-matched, local and global SHAP fall below chance (0.37--0.49), in all seeds (0 of 16 seed-by-edit runs above 0.5 for either method). Causal recovery is more informative. In the table (random distractors, seed 42) it is positive for local SHAP in all four edits ($+0.19$ to $+0.36$) and for global SHAP in three ($-0.03$ to $+0.28$). Among active candidates over seeds 0--3, $\rho$ is positive for local and global SHAP in every seed (per-edit means $0.27$--$0.36$ local, $0.11$--$0.38$ global), but local SHAP's edge over global SHAP is absent or reversed except for appended insertion (removal $0.27$ vs.\ $0.32$, interior insertion $0.36$ vs.\ $0.37$, substitution $0.31$ vs.\ $0.38$, final insertion $0.28$ vs.\ $0.11$), so we read it as weak evidence that $|$attribution$|$ tracks ablation effect, not that attribution identifies the changed features.

A hand-built sentiment lexicon is a weak baseline (accuracy 0.611), but a full-vocabulary bag-of-words logistic regression is not: it reaches 0.794--0.806 against the SAE probe's 0.810, with differences of $+0.003$ to $+0.016$ whose intervals include zero (Appendix~\ref{app:lexical}). SST-2 sentiment is therefore largely recoverable from word presence, and we do not claim the probe carries signal beyond it. The probe also trails a logistic regression on the dense layer-7 residual (0.870). The earlier lexicon-correlation test was uninformative, since the lexicon explains almost none of any top feature's variance ($R^2\approx0.003$). Full-vocabulary bag-of-words explains more (median $R^2$ $0.17$ for the top-20 features against $0.11$ for other masked features; $4/20$ at or above $0.25$), and explains $0.39$ of the probe logit's variance. Top features are thus neither simple lexicon detectors nor free of word identity. Because the task is largely lexical, it gives interaction-aware attribution little room to differ from simpler methods, which limits what these experiments can show.
