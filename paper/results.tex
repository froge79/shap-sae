\subsection{Causal Faithfulness}
Table~\ref{tab:all-selection-faithfulness-last} reports global faithfulness across all 114 masked features under last-token steering, BH-corrected within arm. Importance faithfulness is statistically significant for SHAP, IG, and GA in both arms but small-to-moderate in size ($\rho_{\text{imp}} = 0.23$--$0.33$, $q < 0.03$), so each method explains only a small share of the variation in feature effects. The zero-cost baseline is comparable in the linear arm (probe weights, $\rho_{\text{imp}} = 0.270$) but not in the MLP arm (saliency, $\rho_{\text{imp}} = 0.032$). Directional faithfulness is weaker: SHAP, IG, and GA are near zero and nonsignificant in both arms, while probe weights alone reach $\rho_{\text{dir}} = 0.305$ in the linear arm. This is notable because attribution adds instance-specific information (the feature’s activation relative to the baseline) to the same weights, yet predicts the direction of intervention effects worse than the weights do. One untested explanation is that steering adds a fixed shift, so the sign of its effect follows the sign of the weight. Attribution multiplies each weight by an activation deviation $(x_j - \text{baseline}_j)$ that can change sign across examples, which adds noise to the direction. This noise is not present in the weights. Overall, 5/8 linear-arm and 3/7 MLP-arm tests survive FDR correction (the five linear survivors include SHAP, IG, and GA counted separately; they have identical $p$-values by construction, so 3/4 distinct tests). The importance and directional conclusions do not depend on how the test family is defined (Appendix~\ref{app:bh}). Pooling every global test (both arms, both scopes, duplicates removed) into one family leaves the same 12 of 22 tests significant. Pooling all 32 inferential tests in the paper into one family gives the same 12 survivors. Under Benjamini--Yekutieli, which allows arbitrary dependence, 11 survive; the one that drops is last-token MLP SHAP importance ($q=0.14$). No directional test of SHAP, IG, or GA has $q<0.25$ under any family.

\subsection{Local Faithfulness}
Figure~\ref{fig:local-vs-global} outlines local vs. global SHAP causal faithfulness results. Before the activity control (Block A), local SHAP ranks importance better than $\Phi$ in both arms. The MLP-arm gap has an uncorrected $p=0.024$, but it does not survive FDR correction ($q=0.096$ within the local family, $0.055$ with all inferential tests pooled), and the linear-arm gap does not reach $p<0.05$. With the control (Block B), the importance gap reverses sign in both arms without reaching significance, and no directional gap is significant in either block. We therefore find no evidence that local SHAP is more faithful than global $\Phi$ among active features.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/local_vs_global_faithfulness.png}
\caption{Local vs.\ global attribution, per-example faithfulness ($n=100$ sentences, ablation). \label{fig:local-vs-global}

Our full local-versus-global results are in Table~\ref{tab:app-local-vs-global}. In Block A, local attribution scored higher than global attribution on importance faithfulness (gap $+0.294$ linear, $p=0.064$; $+0.396$ MLP, $p=0.024$), with no directional difference. The active fraction of candidates explained essentially none of the between-example variation in the Block A importance gap ($R^2<0.001$ in both arms; $0.002$ and $0.025$ controlling for candidate-set size). In Block B, restricting to active features reversed the importance gap in both arms ($-0.305$ and $-0.310$), though neither crossed-bootstrap CI excludes zero.

\paragraph{Why global directional attribution may fail.} For a linear probe, $\Phi_j = w_j(\bar{x}_j^{\text{eval}} - \mathbb{E}_{\text{bg}}[x_j])$, so global attribution mostly reflects how the evaluation rows differ from the background rather than feature importance. Its magnitude tracks the size of this mean shift more closely than the size of the weights ($\rho = 0.85$ vs.\ $0.42$ over the non-zero-weight features), and summed over features it totals only $+0.24$ logits. Only 40\% of the top-20 $|\Phi|$ features have $\Phi$ matching the sign of their weight. The background's class balance (49\% positive versus 51.5\% in the evaluation rows) does not by itself predict these sign mismatches, so they more plausibly reflect per-feature differences between the small background and the evaluation rows, which we did not separate from sampling noise. This plausibly explains the near-zero global directional faithfulness. Recomputing $\Phi$ against a larger background would test it.

\subsection{Known Ground-Truth Validation and Lexical Simplicity Control}
Table~\ref{tab:minimal-pairs} reports 100 minimal pairs for each of four edits that cross type with position relative to the final token. The readout tracks the known direction in all four (sign accuracy 0.76--0.96), but it is weakest for removal and interior insertion, where effects sit close to a null-edit noise floor (Appendix~\ref{app:minimal-pairs}). Against random distractors, local SHAP is modestly above chance (AUROC 0.49--0.62) and global SHAP/IG/GA are near chance (0.41--0.51). The apparent insertion advantage for local SHAP is a position effect: appending the clause (0.622) beats placing it mid-sentence (0.492), while removal and interior insertion are alike. Same-seed differences over seeds 0--3 agree (insertion minus interior insertion $+0.12 \pm 0.03$, positive in 4/4; removal minus interior insertion $+0.01 \pm 0.02$). Feature recovery is also not shown to exceed what activity alone gives. The attribution-free baseline ``active at the edited last token'' reaches AUROC 0.79--0.85, above every method, and once distractors are activity-matched, local and global SHAP fall below chance (0.38--0.49), in all seeds (0 of 15 seed-by-edit runs above 0.5 for either method). Causal recovery is more informative: among active candidates $\rho$ is positive for local and global SHAP in every seed ($0.15$--$0.36$), but local SHAP's edge over global SHAP largely disappears except for insertion, so we read it as weak evidence that $|$attribution$|$ tracks ablation effect, not that attribution identifies the changed features.

A hand-built sentiment lexicon is a weak baseline (accuracy 0.611), but a full-vocabulary bag-of-words logistic regression is not: it reaches 0.794--0.806 against the SAE probe's 0.810, with differences of $+0.003$ to $+0.016$ whose intervals include zero (Appendix~\ref{app:lexical}). SST-2 sentiment is therefore largely recoverable from word presence, and we do not claim the probe carries signal beyond it. The probe also trails a logistic regression on the dense layer-7 residual (0.870). The earlier lexicon-correlation test was uninformative, since the lexicon explains almost none of any top feature's variance ($R^2\approx0.003$). Full-vocabulary bag-of-words explains more (median $R^2$ $0.17$ for the top-20 features against $0.11$ for other masked features; $4/20$ at or above $0.25$), and explains $0.39$ of the probe logit's variance. Top features are thus neither simple lexicon detectors nor free of word identity. Because the task is largely lexical, it gives interaction-aware attribution little room to differ from simpler methods, which limits what these experiments can show.
